# Deploying the frozen Alma models on October and November 2026

*4 November 2026. Inference only: no model was retrained, fine-tuned, re-selected or re-weighted. Generated tables are in
[`runs/deploy_2026_10_11/RESULTS.md`](../runs/deploy_2026_10_11/RESULTS.md), and the audit report is in
[`runs/deploy_2026_10_11/AUDIT.md`](../runs/deploy_2026_10_11/AUDIT.md). Every number below appears in those files or in
`runs/deploy_2026_10_11/**.json`; `tools/deploy/check_doc_numbers.py` checks this.*

## 0. Summary

1. **The WM component** is the world-model core: a latent state that a learned transition steps forward in time.
   Forecasts are read from the stepped-forward states (section 1). The question that matters is whether that
   rollout beats its matched "direct twin". The twin has the same modules, parameters and data but carries no state
   forward. On TEST the rollout has not beaten its twin in any project. October 2026 is consistent with that: in
   every project the rollout and its twin are within noise.
2. **Deployment.** All frozen checkpoints were run as-is on the two months after the end of the training period, with
   the data rebuilt from public sources:
   - **Last month** is October 2026, i.e. targets dated 2026-10-01 … 10-31.
   - **This month** is November 2026 to date. That is 2 WTI prints, 5 power delivery days and 3 crypto days. No
     gas release has happened yet, so the first gas forecast is sealed and waits for the 5 November EIA report.
3. **Benchmark.** Each model was compared with every competitor in its project's pre-registration: statistical, EPF,
   tree and foundation models (Chronos-2, Chronos-Bolt, TiRex). That makes 9 to 15 models per project.
   - **Gas:** `wm_gas_ens` has the best quantile score in October from both origins.
   - **Crypto:** the world-model family (`wm_fin`, its twin, and the blend) has the best quantile score.
   - **Power:** `wm_power` has the best MAE but is 4th on quantile score, behind three foundation models.
   - **Oil:** `wm_sv` ties the random walk at 1 day, as on TEST.
   - **Significance:** none of these differences is statistically established. One month holds at most 22–31
     scoring days, or 5 gas weeks, and the harness refuses a verdict below 10 independent units.
4. **Integrity.** An independent audit harness ran **57 of 57 checks, all passed** (section 4; re-run on 5 Nov after the overnight validation strengthened it):
   - checkpoint and code pins;
   - no parameter changed;
   - realised values re-derived from the raw files;
   - every metric recomputed;
   - future-scramble leak tests on all four world models, with 0 forecasts changed;
   - reproduction of the previously audited TEST forecasts.

   The audit also raised five data findings (section 5). None is a leak or a fabricated number. One is a small bug
   in the forward-test scorer.

## 1. What is the "WM" component?

"WM" is short for **world model**. The term appears in two places in the repository:

- `wm/` is the research package of world-model *families* (GRU, RSSM, SSM, Koopman, JEPA, flow, VQ-tokeniser,
  continuous-time …). They share one harness (`train_wm.py`, [`docs/wm_families.md`](wm_families.md)).
- `tools/<project>/wm_*.py` are the frozen *project* world models that were deployed here.

Every Alma world model has the same three parts:

| Part | What it does | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Encoder** | compresses the observed history into a latent state z₀ | e.g. `GasNet.enc` (`tools/gas/search/nets.py`) |
| **Transition (the WM component proper)** | a learned recurrent cell (GRUCell, RSSM, SSM) that advances the state one physical time step at a time: z_{t+1} = f(z_t, u_t), where u_t is the known input for that step (weather, calendar …). The state is *carried* from step to step. | `GasNet.run_days`, `tools/power/wm_models.py`, `tools/fin/wm_core.py`, `tools/commodity/wm_models.py` |
| **Readout** | turns each simulated state into the forecast distribution, often with physics built in | heads below |

How each frozen model rolls its state, and what its twin removes:

| Project | Model | State steps | Physics in the readout | Matched direct twin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gas storage | `wm_gas` | 1 day × 7 days of week W−1 (data assimilation: the error against the released ΔS is written into the state, Kalman-style), then 7 days of the target week | regional injection monotone in HDD/CDD, anchored at the 5-year average; L48 = sum of 5 regions (mass balance) | `wm_gas_direct`: every day is one GRU step from z₀ |
| Power | `wm_power` | 1 hour × 48 hours from the end of D−2 | residual-load head + monotone merit-order price curve, 3 regimes | `wm_power_direct` |
| Crypto vol | `wm_fin` | RSSM, four 6-hour prior sub-steps per day, stochastic latent | quantiles of next-day log realised vol | `wm_direct`: the heads read the D−1 filtered state |
| Oil | `wm_sv` | 1 trading day per step; the latent level and log-volatility are sampled forward | stochastic-volatility block; quantiles from simulated paths | `wm_sv_direct`: decodes all horizons at once |

**Why the twin matters.** The mission ([`MISSION.md`](../MISSION.md) §6) says a model is not a world model just
because it has a latent vector. The rollout has to *earn* its place. Each twin keeps the parameter count, data, steps
and seeds and removes only the carried state, so any gap between the two measures the dynamics alone. On the sealed
TEST year the twin was equal or better in all four projects. The October numbers point the same way:

| Rollout vs twin, October 2026 | Rollout | Direct twin |
|---|---|---|
| Gas, MAE thu / fri (Bcf) | 8.19 / 8.08 | 8.63 / 8.72 |
| Power, QS (EUR/MWh) | 13.10 | 13.31 |
| Crypto, QS (`corrected` data) | 0.1490 | 0.1478 |
| Oil, 1-day QS | 0.00932 | 0.00931 |

These are 5 weeks, 31 days, 31 days and 22 days of data, all inside noise. The world-model test remains **not passed**.

## 2. What was deployed, on what data

**Timeline.**
- Training data ended on 2025-06-30, or 2022-12-31 for oil.
- VAL ran to 2025-10-31, and the sealed TEST year was 2025-11-01 … 2026-10-31.
- The forward-test freeze was on 2026-11-01.

So October 2026 is 15+ months after training ended, but it is **the last month of the TEST year, which has
already been scored**. November 2026 is new data that no model, threshold or blend weight has seen.

**Models.** The checkpoints were loaded exactly as frozen in `winning_models/`; their sha256 is checked before and
after every run. Competitors kept their pre-registered protocols. For example, LEAR refits its lasso each day on the
history available at the origin, and OLS/HAR were fitted once on the training period by their own code. That is how
those baselines are defined, not extra training of Alma.

## 4. The integrity harness

`tools/deploy/` has two layers, and the audit layer imports nothing from the deploy layer.

**Guards inside every deploy run** (`common.py`):
- `no_training()` makes every torch optimizer step and every `backward()` raise for the whole process.
- The parameters of every loaded network are hashed at load and at the end, and the hashes must match.
- Fin models must report 0 online updates.
- Checkpoints are checked against `SHA256SUMS` and `tools/forward/FREEZE.json` before and after the run.
- The run refuses to start if frozen code has changed.
- No "better/worse" verdict is issued on fewer than 10 independent scoring units. Overlapping h-day targets and
  7-day block dependence are counted.

**Independent audit** (`audit_deploy.py`, 57 of 57 checks passed; see `AUDIT.md`):

| | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A | checkpoint/code pins, git shows no edits to frozen files | 29 FREEZE files + 18 checkpoints unchanged |
| B | no optimizer/backward in deploy code; parameter hashes unchanged; 0 online updates | all runs |
| C | realised values re-derived from raw files with separate parsers | WTI csv, Energy-Charts JSON (max diff 5.68e-14), EIA xls, Binance zips (max diff 8.9e-16) |
| C | earlier audited forecasts reproduced | gas TEST 5.1e-05 Bcf; crypto October 4.8e-07 |
| D | every metric recomputed from the saved forecasts | oil 54/54, power 52/52, gas 104/104, crypto 60/60 + 116/116 |
| E | future-scramble leak tests: every value at/after the target time replaced by noise | 0/30 oil, 0/12 power, 0/30 gas, 0/6 crypto forecasts changed |
| F | no significance claim on too few units | 0 violations |
| G | forecast files sha256-sealed; ex-ante targets listed | gas 2026-11-05 |
| R | each saved forecast equals a fresh re-run of the frozen model (added 5 Nov) | exact for oil, gas, crypto; power within 6.1e-05 EUR/MWh |

**Corrections from the overnight validation (5 Nov).** The validation suite in `tools/validate/` tested the harness itself and found four defects, all fixed and re-verified (`runs/validation/FINDINGS.json`). (1) The audit did not re-run the models, so a perfectly consistent fake forecast file could pass: section R now does. (2) The gas leak check (section E) re-queried a model that memoises by week, so its "0 changed" result was guaranteed: the check now clears the memo, has a positive control, and still finds 0 changed. (3) The no-training lock did not block a concrete optimizer's step (backward() was blocked, so nothing could train): it now blocks optimizer construction too. (4) The checkpoint check covered the published copies, not the copies the wrappers load: it now covers both (they were byte-identical). None of these changes a reported forecast or score. Full report: `reports/Alma_Models_Validation_Report_2026-11-05.pdf`.

To re-run everything, use `tools/deploy/run_all.sh`. To grade the sealed forecasts after publication, use
`tools/deploy/score_exante.py`; it refuses to score if a sealed file changed.

## 5. Findings raised by the audit

1. **October 2026 crypto funding rates were missing from the pinned TEST data** and were silently set to 0. Binance
   published the October monthly funding files on 2026-11-01, after the data was frozen, and the gap is not
   recorded in the manifest's `missing` list. `wm_fin`, `lgbm_dow` and `lgbm` read funding. I re-ran October with
   the real funding on identical coin-days (`fin/funding_effect.json`): `wm_fin` QS went from 0.1430 to 0.1431, so
   the effect is negligible. Models without funding are bit-identical. This is not a leak, and the TEST claims stand.
2. **Binance's archive has no data at all for 2026-10-28**: zero bars for all 24 coins, even in the monthly file.
   Under the official label rule (`harness.Panel.labels`, mean of the 7 calendar days before D) that leaves decision
   days 2026-10-29 … 11-04 without a P1 label. `har_intra_dow`, and so `wm_fin_blend`, also gives no forecast for
   them. **`tools/forward/forward_eval.py` uses a row-based rolling mean instead**, which silently takes D−8 in
   place of the missing day. That is a small mismatch with the pre-registered label in the forward-test scorer.
   This deployment uses the official labels. Fixing `forward_eval.py` is suggested; I did not edit it here, because
   it belongs to the running forward test.
3. **Parquet files are not byte-reproducible across platforms.** The original build ran on Apple arm64 and this one
   on Linux x86-64, so the sha256 pins of `data/fin/*.parquet` differ. The content is equivalent: the October
   `wm_fin` forecasts reproduce the audit's cached TEST forecasts to 4.8e-07.
4. **The power `hourly.csv` pin (amendment A1) cannot be reproduced byte-for-byte from today's downloads.** Its date
   window is not recorded, and the 2026 generation file from Energy-Charts has changed bytes. All 2015–2025 raw files
   and the 2026 price file are identical, and the rebuilt table is a strict extension of the pinned-window table.
5. **Open-Meteo refuses this environment's shared egress IP (HTTP 429)**, so `data/gas/daily.csv` was built without
   its weather columns (`tools/deploy/gas_daily_noweather.py`). The storage models do not read those columns, and the
   exact TEST reproduction confirms they are unaffected. The separate Henry Hub *price* task was not deployed.
