World Models for Time‑Series Forecasting

A verified reading list across the literatures that meet here: world models from reinforcement learning, JEPA, latent state-space models, time-series foundation models, AI weather systems, counterfactual forecasting and market simulators. It also covers the ground under all of them: messy real-world data, forecasting competitions and evaluation, enterprise forecasting platforms and treasury practice.

The short version

What a forecaster needs before it can be called a world model, and where the field actually stands.

Latent state, not raw values

A world model encodes observations into a state, rolls that state forward and decodes only when needed. RSSM (PlaNet, Dreamer), Kalman-style models (RKN, MTS3) and JEPA predictors all share this split.

Actions change the future

Forecasters treat covariates as passive context. World models condition on actions you control. ActTime, TimeWorld, BeliefGraph-JEPA and WhatIf-Bench are the first time-series work to take this seriously, all from 2026.

Today's foundation models are not there yet

Chronos-2, TimesFM and TiRex answer what-if questions by association, not causation. JEPA pretraining helps some backbones and hurts others. Weather models (GenCast, AIFS-CRPS, NeuralGCM) are the most mature working examples.

    Real, but overstated

    Looped World Models (LoopWM)

    The paper exists: arXiv 2606.18208, FaceMind Research, June 2026. It does use a weight-shared transformer block run in a loop to refine the latent state.

    The "100x parameter efficiency" compares a ~1B model against closed chat models whose sizes are guesses. The "spectral stability guarantee" covers only the linear part of the update. It is tested on text environments (ScienceWorld, AlfWorld), not time series.

    Garbled name

    "TAM Gen" (Nixtla / Azure)

    This is TimeGEN-1, Nixtla's TimeGPT hosted in the Azure AI model catalog. It is a pretrained zero-shot forecaster, not a world model. Paper: TimeGPT-1. Azure: deployment guide.

    Accurate

    ECMWF AIFS and GenCast

    Both are real and among the strongest AI weather systems. AIFS-ENS, trained with a CRPS loss, went operational at ECMWF on 1 July 2025. GenCast is a diffusion ensemble that beat ECMWF ENS on 97% of targets (Nature, 2024).

    Open research gaps

    Gaps that came up independently in several of the literatures below. Good places to look for a thesis topic.

    1. No survey of time-series world models. Every world-model survey covers robots, video or games. A taxonomy for time series (latent dynamics, action conditioning, JEPA vs. generative, filtering vs. rollout) does not exist yet.
    2. Action data is scarce. Most datasets log no interventions. Genie-style discovery of latent actions from unlabelled data has never been tried on time series.
    3. Foundation models are associational. No foundation-scale model has been trained to answer interventional questions correctly.
    4. Calibrated uncertainty and scale rarely meet. Kalman-style belief states (RKN, MTS3, KalMamba) have never been benchmarked at foundation-model scale on GIFT-Eval or fev-bench.
    5. JEPA for time series has no reliable recipe. Results swing by backbone. Collapse prevention, masking and horizon conditioning are unsettled.
    6. Looped and recurrent-depth models are untested on standard forecasting benchmarks. LoopWM has no time-series results. Adaptive depth per horizon step is unexplored.
    7. Joint trajectories, not marginal quantiles. A usable simulator needs coherent multi-variable sample paths. PathFM and Sundial are early attempts.
    8. Few models are judged by the decisions they enable. Planning or MPC inside the latent space is rarely evaluated. That matters most for finance and treasury.
    9. Long-horizon drift. Weather models solved it with geometry-aware operators, multi-step training and hard constraints. Those fixes have not been carried over to general time series.
    10. Benchmark contamination. Several leading models are flagged for training on test data. Leak-free suites (TIME, IND-Eval) are new and small.
    11. No public benchmark for messy data. No dataset combines per-channel sampling rates, block missingness and real cross-channel dependence. ChannelTokenFormer's most realistic dataset is proprietary.
    12. Foundation models assume one frequency. Moirai and its successors set frequency per dataset, not per channel. A cash position fed by daily bank data, weekly receivables and monthly macro drivers is out of reach for them zero-shot.
    13. Treasury has almost no academic literature. The strongest papers predict invoice payment dates. Corporate cash forecasting with world models or foundation models is open ground, and most published accuracy figures come from vendors.
